How it works

It comes down to three numbers.

All three come from Bitget data. If selling first doesn't clearly come out ahead, the answer is hold.

01 · THE DROP

The price really does drop

Across 127 past dividends, by 04:00 ET the rToken had dropped by almost the whole dividend.

0.97× the dividend
02 · THE TAX

But you give up the dividend

If you sell, you don't get paid. After up to 30% US tax, that's 70% of the dividend you're walking away from.

70% kept after 30% tax
03 · THE COST

And getting out and back in isn't free

Two fees plus slippage. Whatever's left after the dividend you gave up has to cover this.

~45 bps round trip

Proof

What I found

  • The overnight drop is real. On average it's 0.97× the dividend.
  • On unseen data, Exnight came out 32.5 bps per event ahead of selling first.
  • Selling first lost at every cost I tried (10 to 100 bps) and every tax rate (0% to 30%).
  • Buying just to catch the dividend lost money too, in every setup.
  • Bitget's Playbook backtest doesn't credit dividends, so it makes selling first look better than it is.
  • A real $10 order filled at the quoted price, even with an empty public order book.
  • Every call is in Git before the cutoff, so nobody can change it once the result is in.

What's still open

  • EXIT hasn't fired yet. It needs a big dividend (about 0.7%+) and a confident estimate. The live high-dividend events are its first real shot.
  • Past trading costs are modelled, because old order books aren't available.
  • Trades of $1,000 and up are modelled too. Only the $10 order is real so far.
  • The live test is still going. A few events alone don't prove much.

Quick glossary

Four words you'll see in the app

EX-DATE
The day the price drops by the dividend. You must hold the token before it to get paid.
HOLD
Keep the token through the ex-date. Selling first would cost more than it saves.
EXIT
Sell before the cutoff and buy back after. Only when that clearly wins after tax and costs.
NO_SIGNAL
Not enough to go on, like a trade too big for the visible quotes. It won't guess.

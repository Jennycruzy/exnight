## 1. Question

When a US-listed share goes ex-dividend, its price drops by a fraction of the dividend — about 0.77 in the classic US studies. Bitget rTokens are 1:1-backed tokens of those shares that trade in USDT on a 24/5 venue, with a separate USDT dividend credit net of 30% withholding (clean-price model, DOCUMENTED). Nobody had measured how *that* market prices the ex-date.

H₀: the rToken's price-drop ratio (PDR) equals the underlying's.

## 3. Measurement

rTokens trade continuously through the US overnight, so there is no single "open". The engine takes one pre-event price and a ladder of post-event prices:

- **P_pre:** last 1-minute close strictly before **20:00 ET on the last US trading day before the ex-date** (D−1). That is the US after-hours close — the last moment the token is unambiguously cum-dividend under exchange rules, and the time at which Bitget's own stock perps settle dividends (DOCUMENTED, article 12560603895292).
- **P_post:** first 1-minute close at or after each of: 20:00 ET D−1, 04:00 ET D, 09:30 ET D, 10:00 ET D, 16:00 ET D.
- **PDR = (P_pre − P_post) / gross dividend.**
- **Literature convention**, for the like-for-like comparison: rToken close before 16:00 ET D−1 → first bar at/after 09:30 ET D; underlying close(D−1) → open(D).

**Estimator.** A ratio's noise scales with 1/yield: MU pays $0.15 on a ~$1,000 token (0.015%), so a normal minute of noise becomes a PDR of ±200. The headline estimator is therefore the cross-sectional regression of ex-day return on dividend yield, `r = a − PDR·(D/P) + e`, whose slope is the PDR and whose standard error is honest; every event contributes in proportion to what it can tell us. Means and medians of the raw ratios are shown beside it, with two yield floors, so the reader can see how much the estimator choice moves the answer.

### 4.1 The finding: the dividend is priced out at 20:00 ET, in one minute, at ~1.0× gross

At the first bar after the after-hours close the slope PDR is **1.10 ± 0.12** (n = 59, p = 1e-12). That is **3.3 standard errors above 0.70** (the net dividend holders actually receive) and 0.8 s.e. from 1.00. It is stable across yield floors (1.09–1.12). Event by event, liquid names show it plainly: rBITI 29.16 → 27.73 on a $1.45 dividend (0.99), rSQQQ 0.81, rSCHD 0.79, rPM 1.03, rDIS 0.91, rSPXU 1.07 — all inside the first minute after 20:00 ET.

Every later rung is far noisier (s.e. 0.4–0.6): once the mechanical adjustment has happened, ordinary overnight and intraday variance dominates and the ex-day signal is no longer separable at this sample size.

**Interpretation.** The rToken does not "discover" the ex-dividend price overnight. The reference price is adjusted by the full gross dividend at the after-hours close — the same moment Bitget's perps settle — and trading continues from there. Whether this is a market-maker rule or an exchange-side reference adjustment is not documented; it is OBSERVED behaviour.

**Monday ex-dates are different.** rKO and rMO (Monday ex-dates, both on the weekend-trading list) show no adjustment at Friday 20:00 ET; the drop arrives later. With only a handful of Monday events the timing cannot be pinned down and they are reported, not modelled.

### 4.2 The comparison: on the literature's own convention, rToken and underlying are indistinguishable

Close-to-open, the rToken's slope PDR is 0.90 ± 0.41 and the underlying's is 1.00 ± 0.42 (all events); 0.51 ± 0.46 vs 0.66 ± 0.45 (yield ≥ 0.5%). Both are consistent with the classic ~0.77 and with each other. **H₀ is not rejected on that convention.** The sample cannot distinguish a 0.1 difference in PDR at overnight horizons; it would need several hundred events.

What the sample *can* show is something the literature never could, because the NYSE is closed at 20:00 ET: the ex-date adjustment on the tokenized venue is a discrete, timestamped, ~1.0× step, not a diffuse overnight drift.

### 4.3 Is the 20:00 ET price tradable?

- 37 of 59 events have traded volume in the 30 minutes after 20:00 ET (OBSERVED from candle volume). rSQQQ traded $8.7M, rSPXU $2.2M, rSCHD $0.98M in that half hour; thin names (rHYS, rFTGC, rOPRA) traded nothing and their "price" in that window is a quote.
- Live order-book snapshot at 2026-09-16 11:25 UTC (07:25 ET, pre-market): **43 of 59 books were empty.** Of the 16 quoted, median spread 11 bp, top-of-book depth $130–$21k. This is one snapshot at one hour and is labelled OBSERVED-NOW; it is not the book at any event. It says that for most of the sample, on-platform depth outside US hours is small or absent.

## 5. Regime verdict (spec §0.6)

Measured at the moment the adjustment happens, **PDR ≈ 1.1 > 0.70: holding through the ex-date loses ≈ (0.70 − 1.1) × D ≈ −0.4 D per share before costs.** That is the **EXIT** regime: sell before 20:00 ET on D−1, re-enter after. Measured close-to-open, the PDR is ≈0.7–0.9 with s.e. ≈0.4 and no regime can be asserted.

Whether the EXIT edge survives costs depends on yield: 0.4 × yield must exceed two fees (2 × 0.05% during the promotional period, 2 × 0.10% now), the spread crossed twice, and slippage against books that are often empty. For a 1% yield that is ~0.4% gross against ~0.3% of costs at today's spreads; for the median event (yield ≈ 0.3%) it is negative. **The strategy engine (C3) will make that call per event, and most events will be HOLD.** That is the expected shape of the result, and it is decided by the numbers above, not in advance.

## 6. What this does not establish

- **Eligibility.** Bitget's snapshot time is not published ("at the time of the snapshot"). `eligibility_verified` is false on every event and every BUY signal is suppressed. The EXIT signal does not depend on eligibility (it avoids the dividend), which is why it can be evaluated at all.
- **Confounders.** No event has yet been screened for contemporaneous news (M0 §3.4 is build step 6, after this gate). The 20:00 ET rung is a one-minute window, which limits contamination; the later rungs are not protected.
- **Spread at event.** No historical order book exists. Costs in C3 will use the live snapshot and say so.
- **Monday ex-dates and weekend sessions.** Too few events to characterise.
- **Sample period.** Six weeks, one payment batch, promotional fee period, a three-month-old product. Later batches were not found in the announcement API (it returns only recent items); if they exist they should be added to the ledger.
- **In-place split adjustment** (a split without a re-listing) remains ASSUMED; no such case exists in the sample.

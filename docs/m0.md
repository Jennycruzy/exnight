Rendered by `python scripts/render_m0.py` from `data/results/`, which `python -m exnight.calendar && python -m exnight.eventstudy && python -m exnight.analysis` regenerate from the saved sources and raw candles. `tests/test_m0_figures.py` asserts the figures quoted here against the results.

Labels: **DOCUMENTED** (first-party Bitget page, saved and checksummed), **OBSERVED** (we ran it, date given), **ASSUMED** (unverified). The verification trail is `docs/verification.md`.

## 1. Question

When a US share goes ex-dividend its price drops by a fraction of the dividend — about 0.77 in the classic US studies. Bitget rTokens are 1:1-backed tokens of those shares trading in USDT on a 24/5 venue, with the dividend paid separately in USDT net of 30% withholding (clean-price model, DOCUMENTED). Nobody had measured how *that* market prices the ex-date.

H₀: the rToken's price-drop ratio (PDR) equals the underlying's.

## 3. Measurement

rTokens trade continuously through the US overnight, so there is no single open. The engine takes one pre-event price and a ladder of post-event prices.

- **P_pre:** last 1-minute close strictly before **20:00 ET on the last NYSE trading day before the ex-date** (weekends and NYSE holidays skipped; Bitget printed bars on both in-sample holidays, so the calendar is applied explicitly). 20:00 ET is the US after-hours close — the last moment the token is unambiguously cum-dividend — and, in summer, 00:00 UTC, the time at which Bitget settles dividends on its stock perps (DOCUMENTED, article 12560603895292; whether the rToken adjustment is keyed to 20:00 ET or to 00:00 UTC cannot be told apart in a summer-only sample).
- **P_post:** first 1-minute close at or after each of 20:00 ET (D−1), 04:00, 09:30, 10:00 and 16:00 ET on D. **PDR = (P_pre − P_post) / gross dividend.**
- The 20:00 rung is only a measurement of the adjustment itself when the pre and post bars are adjacent. The gap is recorded per event: 34 of 60 events have pre→post ≤ 5 minutes; 12 events are Monday-type (ex-date after a weekend or holiday), where that rung spans the whole weekend. The **clean subset** is gap ≤ 5 min and not Monday-type: n = 28.
- **Literature convention** for the like-for-like comparison: rToken close before 16:00 ET D−1 → first bar at/after 09:30 ET D; underlying close(D−1) → open(D).
- **Estimator.** A ratio's noise scales with 1/yield: MU pays $0.15 on a ~$1,000 token (0.015%), so one ordinary minute becomes a PDR of ±200. The headline is therefore the cross-sectional regression of ex-day return on dividend yield, `r = a − PDR·(D/P) + e`, whose slope is the PDR. Because a slope can be carried by a few high-yield points, every table reports the leave-one-out range, the slope without the three highest-yield events, and the ratio of sums. Means and medians of the raw ratios are shown beside it.

## 5. What the sample supports

**A discrete adjustment at the after-hours close, of roughly the gross dividend, is visible in the liquid higher-yield ETFs.** rBITI 29.16 → 27.73 on a $1.45 dividend (0.99), rSQQQ 0.81, rSCHD 0.79, rSPXU 1.07, rPSQ 0.70, rPM 1.03, rDIS 0.91 — each in the first bar after 20:00 ET. On the clean weekday subset the pooled slope is 1.01 ± 0.13 (n = 20), with a leave-one-out range of [0.88, 1.02].

**That precision comes from three leveraged ETFs.** Without rSPXU, rSQQQ and rBITI the clean-subset slope is 0.79 ± 0.60; on all events, without rHIMX, rOPRA and rBITI it is 0.76 ± 0.22. The ratio-of-sums estimator gives 0.66 on the clean subset and 0.31 on all events. **Once the three most informative events are removed the sample cannot distinguish 0.70 from 1.00.**

**Monday-type ex-dates are not adjusted at Friday 20:00 ET.** rKO and rMO show exactly 0.00 at that rung and the drop appears by Monday pre-market. With 12 such events and no weekend bars for most of them, the timing cannot be located more precisely.

**On the literature's own convention the rToken and the underlying are indistinguishable.** Close-to-open, all events: rToken **0.98 ± 0.41** vs underlying **1.01 ± 0.42**. Both are consistent with the classic ~0.77 and with each other. **H₀ is not rejected.** Overnight variance is far larger than the dividend for almost every event; separating a 0.1 difference in PDR at that horizon would need several hundred events.

**Liquidity.** No historical order book exists. A live snapshot at 2026-09-16 11:25 UTC (07:25 ET) found **43 of 59 sample books empty**; the 16 quoted showed median spread 11 bp and top-of-book depth of $118–$20,919. One snapshot at one hour, labelled OBSERVED-NOW. During US hours orders may route to US liquidity that never appears in this book (DOCUMENTED, article 12560603887176), so an empty book outside US hours is the relevant fact for an overnight strategy, not a general one.

## 6. Regime verdict (spec §0.6)

The point estimate at the moment of adjustment is ≈1.0, above the 0.70 that holders net — the **EXIT** regime: sell before 20:00 ET on D−1, re-enter after. But the evidence that the PDR exceeds 0.70 rests on three events, and every other estimator or subset is consistent with 0.70. **The verdict is "EXIT indicated, not established."** The strategy engine must carry the PDR as a distribution, not a number; net of two fees, a crossed spread and books that are often empty, the minimum-edge gate will return HOLD for most events. That is decided by these figures, not in advance.

## 7. Not established here

- **Eligibility.** Bitget's snapshot time is unpublished ("at the time of the snapshot"); `eligibility_verified` is false on every event and BUY signals are suppressed. EXIT does not depend on eligibility, which is why it can be evaluated.
- **Confounders.** No event is yet screened for contemporaneous news (build step 6). The 20:00 rung's one-minute window limits contamination on the clean subset; later rungs are unprotected.
- **Spread at event.** Unknowable; costs will use the live snapshot and say so.
- **Monday-type ex-dates and weekend sessions.** Too few events.
- **Sample period.** Six weeks, one payment batch, promotional 0.05% fee period, a three-month-old product. The public announcement API returns only recent items; later batches, if any, are not in the ledger.
- **In-place split adjustment** (a split without a re-listing) remains ASSUMED; no such case exists.
